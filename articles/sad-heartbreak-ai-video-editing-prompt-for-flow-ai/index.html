Sad Heartbreak AI Video Editing Prompt For Flow AI

By Prompt Library • 18 September 2026

Sometimes a simple video can express emotions that are difficult to explain in words.

If you want to create a sad, emotional, and cinematic heartbreak video using your own photo, this Flow AI prompt can help you create that type of video.

In this tutorial, I will share a Sad Heartbreak AI Video Editing Prompt for Flow AI that creates a cinematic night-time highway scene. The main character walks alone, looks at a photo of someone special on his phone, and spends some quiet moments thinking.

What Is This Sad Heartbreak AI Video Prompt?

This is a cinematic AI video concept made for Flow AI. It focuses on loneliness, heartbreak, and quiet emotions rather than dramatic action.

The video contains seven connected scenes. The man first looks at a woman’s photo on his phone. After that, he walks alone on an empty highway, sits on a divider, looks at the night sky, and finally stands on the road while vehicle headlights appear in the background.

The important part is consistency. The same man’s face, hairstyle, body build, clothes, highway, lighting, and color grading should remain consistent from the first scene to the last.

What You Need Before Creating the Video

You will need two reference photos.

Photo 1:
Use a clear photo of the man who will appear in the video. Try to use a photo where his face and hairstyle are clearly visible.

Photo 2:
Use the woman’s photo that you want to display on the phone screen.

The woman’s photo should appear only on the phone screen. She should not appear as a separate live character in the video.

How To Create This Heartbreak Video in Flow AI

Follow these simple steps.

Step 1: Prepare Your Reference Photos

First, select your two photos.

Photo 1 should be the main character’s reference image. Make sure the face is clear and the photo is not heavily edited.

Photo 2 will be used as the image displayed on the phone in the first scene.

Keeping good-quality reference photos will usually make it easier for the AI to maintain visual consistency.

Step 2: Open Flow AI

Open Flow AI and start a new video generation project.

Upload Photo 1 as the reference for the male character.

When the tool allows additional image references, add Photo 2 for the phone-screen image.

The main character should be based only on Photo 1.

Step 3: Use the Complete Prompt

Now copy the prompt below and paste it into Flow AI.

Sad Heartbreak AI Video Prompt

Use Photo 1 as reference for the man’s face, hairstyle, skin tone, body build and height. Use Photo 2 as reference for the woman — her photo appears only as an image displayed on a phone screen, not as a live character. Keep the man’s identity exactly same as his reference photo — no alteration to face, body, or hairstyle.

Keep the person’s body proportions natural and realistic, matching the exact build shown in the reference photo.

Outfit (same throughout, do not change): dark green zip-up jacket, black pants, sandals.

Location (same throughout all 7 scenes, do not change): an empty night-time highway/bridge, glowing teal-green streetlights lining the road, black-and-white striped concrete road dividers, dry grass median, a foggy dark sky, occasional distant vehicle headlights, moody nighttime atmosphere.

Mood: heartbroken, quiet, reflective, sad expression throughout, slow melancholic body language.

Color grade: cinematic cool teal-and-dark tone, moody night contrast, cinematic film-grain look.

Step 4: Generate the Scenes

After adding the prompt, generate the video.

Because this prompt contains seven different scenes, the result may vary depending on how Flow AI handles multi-scene generation.

If the tool generates scenes separately, create each scene using the corresponding scene description while keeping the character and location references unchanged.

This is especially useful when you want better control over the final video.

Step 5: Maintain Character Consistency

Character consistency is one of the most important parts of this type of AI video.

If the face changes between scenes, the video may not look like the same person.

To reduce this problem, keep repeating the important instructions:

  • Use Photo 1 as the main identity reference.
  • Keep the same hairstyle.
  • Keep the same skin tone.
  • Keep the same body proportions.
  • Keep the same dark green jacket.
  • Keep the same black pants.
  • Keep the same sandals.
  • Keep the same nighttime highway environment.

Avoid adding unnecessary changes between scenes.

Step 6: Keep the Same Cinematic Look

The visual style is also important.

The prompt uses a cool teal and dark color grade with cinematic contrast and subtle film grain.

This gives the video a darker nighttime feeling and helps connect all seven scenes visually.

Try not to change the lighting style or environment between scenes. The highway, streetlights, fog, road dividers, and dry grass should feel like they belong to the same place.

Scene-by-Scene Video Idea

Here is what the final video is supposed to feel like.

Scene 1 – Looking At Her Photo

The video starts with a close-up of a phone in someone’s hand.

The woman’s photo is visible on the phone screen while the night highway remains softly blurred in the background.

This scene introduces the emotional story without showing the woman as a real person in the scene.

Scene 2 – Standing Alone

The camera moves to a wide shot.

The man is standing alone on the empty highway with his head slightly down. The teal-green streetlights create a cinematic background while the road disappears into the distance.

Scene 3 – Walking Alone

The man starts walking slowly along the road.

His hands remain relaxed and his eyes are directed downward. The road markings and streetlights create strong leading lines behind him.

Scene 4 – Sitting On The Divider

Next, he sits on the concrete divider.

His elbows rest on his knees while he looks down. Dry grass and the streetlight behind him add more depth to the shot.

Scene 5 – Looking At The Sky

The mood becomes slightly calmer.

The man leans back and looks toward the dark sky. This creates a quiet moment where the character appears lost in his thoughts.

Scene 6 – Leaning Against The Barrier

The next shot shows him standing near the road barrier.

His head is bowed and his hands are clasped in front of him. Keep the lighting dim and shadowy to maintain the melancholic atmosphere.

Scene 7 – Final Highway Shot

The final scene uses a wide composition.

The man faces the camera with his arms crossed. Distant vehicle headlights appear behind him while the night highway continues into the background.

The final shot should feel calm, emotional, and cinematic rather than overly dramatic.

Tips For A Better Result

If your first generation does not look exactly right, don’t immediately change the entire prompt.

Instead, identify the problem.

If the face changes, strengthen the identity-reference instruction.

If the clothes change, repeat the exact outfit description.

If the location changes, clearly mention that the same highway environment must remain unchanged.

If the mood becomes too happy, reinforce the quiet and melancholic expression.

If the phone photo looks incorrect, make it clear that Photo 2 must appear only as a photograph displayed on the phone screen.

Another useful tip is to generate the scenes individually when possible. You can then combine the clips later in an editor such as CapCut or VN.

Final Result

With the right reference photos and consistent generation settings, you can create a cinematic heartbreak video that tells a simple emotional story without needing complicated editing.

The combination of an empty highway, nighttime streetlights, slow walking shots, the phone photo, and the dark cinematic color grade gives the video a strong emotional atmosphere.

If you are making this type of AI video for Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, or other social media platforms, you can also experiment with different background music and slow transitions during the final editing stage.
